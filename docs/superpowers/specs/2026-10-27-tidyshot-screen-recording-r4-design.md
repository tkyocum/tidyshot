# TidyShot screen recording R4: design (2026-10-27)

R4 finishes the screen recording list. R1's design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-tidyshot-screen-recording-design.md`, R2's is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-tidyshot-screen-recording-r2-design.md` and R3's is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-24-tidyshot-screen-recording-r3-design.md`; everything there still holds unless this document changes it. R1 to R3's decisions stand. The coordinator code goes where the coordinator split design's section 6 places it (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-coordinator-split-design.md`): the URL entry points and the Settings deferral reason in `AppCoordinator+Recording.swift`; multipart's events, Clop copy and Retry in `+Upload.swift`, its abort in `+Delete.swift`; the drag-out download in `+DragOut.swift`, with one change each in `+RecordActions.swift` (Save As… waits for it) and `+Overlay.swift` (the card's status). New stored state goes in the class body in `AppCoordinator.swift`, under its area's group. The research behind this design was done in `.superpowers/sdd/r4/`, which is local scratch and not in the repository; this document restates everything it uses from it.

**The scope (`.superpowers/sdd/r4/decisions.md`, the controller's call, for Tim to overrule).**
1. **URL commands `record-screen` and `stop-recording`**, behind the same setting and gates as the capture commands. `record-screen` acts like the Record Screen shortcut's start. It never starts a recording silently: Screen Recording, the microphone and the camera keep their consent rules, and the selection shows unless a parameter names the area.
2. **S3 multipart upload with resume** for large recordings, with TidyShot's own SigV4 signing and no new package. Today a recording over 5 GiB is not uploaded.
3. **Drag-out of an uploaded video with no local file** as a download with progress into the drag folder, under 2.9's container-copy rule.
4. **Small:** a Settings prompt deferred while a GIF is being saved logs `a capture is running`, which is false; it gets a true reason.

The stale-upload question (an edit saved locally leaves the pre-edit capture uploaded) stays open: nothing is built for it. No pause and no separate upload lane (R2, Left out).

Every choice below that the scope does not settle is marked **(the design's call)**, and the list at the end collects them so Tim can overrule any one. Type names, log wording and constants that are implementation details are not marked.

## Probes (sources go in `docs/superpowers/research/recording-probes/`, listed in its README under R4)

Each probe talks to Tim's bucket or needs his screen, so each is Tim's to run. No probe records the screen or opens the camera or the microphone. The two network probes read `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY` and an optional `AWS_SESSION_TOKEN` from the environment of the shell that runs them, use them for that run only and never write them anywhere. Build each with `xcrun swiftc -O -target arm64-apple-macos26.0 -o /tmp/<name> <name>.swift TidyShot/Upload/S3Signer.swift TidyShot/Upload/S3DestinationConfig.swift TidyShot/Upload/AWSCredentials.swift` from the repository root (the probe carries its own small request code, so it compiles with no other app file). Results go in this section, with the date and the Mac, as R2's and R3's did.

- **M1, multipart against `snap.tky.io`** (`r4-multipart.swift <bucket> <region> <endpoint> <prefix>`; a real upload, Tim's to run). Under `<prefix>/r4-probe-<random>` it runs, in order, and prints each request's status and the time it took: CreateMultipartUpload with `Content-Type: video/mp4`; two UploadParts of 5 MiB each (part 1 and 2, payload signed with its SHA-256) and one of 1 MiB (part 3), three at a time; ListParts; CompleteMultipartUpload with the three ETags, printing the whole body (a 200 can carry `<Error>`); HEAD of the key and its `Content-Length` (11 MiB expected); DELETE. Then a second upload with one part, and AbortMultipartUpload on it; ListParts on the aborted id (404 `NoSuchUpload` expected); and a Complete on the aborted id (404 expected). Then a 2 GiB sparse file (`FileHandle.truncate`) uploaded in 16 MiB parts, 3 in flight, with `footprint <pid>` read by Tim at the halfway mark, then deleted. Expected: every status in the 2xx range, except the 404s named; ListParts lists the three parts with their sizes and ETags; the HEAD size matches; the footprint stays under 120 MB. A 403 on ListParts or on Abort is written down with the body's `<Code>`: it means Tim's IAM policy lacks `s3:ListMultipartUploadParts` or `s3:AbortMultipartUpload` (G1).
- **D1, a signed download with progress and resume** (`r4-download.swift <bucket> <region> <endpoint> <key>`, on an object of at least 100 MB that Tim names, for example M1's 2 GiB object before its DELETE, or any uploaded recording). It downloads through `URLSessionDownloadTask` with a task delegate and prints each `didWriteData` as a fraction with its time; at 50 % it calls `cancel(byProducingResumeData:)`, prints whether resume data came back and how large it is, waits 2 s, resumes with `downloadTask(withResumeData:)` and prints the first fraction after the resume and the final status; then it waits 16 minutes with no request, resumes again from the same resume data, and prints the status (403 expected, the signature having expired). Expected: progress callbacks arrive and move; the first resume carries on from about 50 % with a 206; the file's size equals `Content-Length`; the late resume is refused (G2).
- **U1, the URL commands by hand** (no probe source; the checklist). `open 'tidyshot://record-screen'` from Terminal, from Raycast and from Keyboard Maestro, then `open 'tidyshot://stop-recording'` during the selection, the count-in, the recording and the save; `record-screen` with a rect with the silent toggle off and on; `record-screen` during a recording and during a screenshot's selection; a cold launch from `tidyshot://record-screen`. These record the screen, so they are Tim's and are not probes but hand checks.
- **Setup step, not a probe.** Tim adds a lifecycle rule to `snap.tky.io`, `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` with `DaysAfterInitiation` 7 and an empty prefix, and checks it with `aws s3api get-bucket-lifecycle-configuration --bucket snap.tky.io` (Multipart, Orphaned uploads; the README gets the JSON).

**Gates.** The pure code (Tests), the URL commands and the whole drag-out download build on no probe. These parts wait:
- **G1, multipart against the real bucket** waits on M1. Everything builds and is tested against the local stub first; M1 confirms before the PR merges that Create, UploadPart, Complete and Abort work with Tim's key and the dotted path-style bucket, that ListParts does, and that a Complete answers with a plain 200. If ListParts is 403, the lost-sidecar fallback (Multipart, Resume) becomes "abort and start again" and the README says why. If Abort is 403, the README's policy gains `s3:AbortMultipartUpload` and Tim adds it before the merge; the app's Delete is not changed to swallow the 403. A Complete whose 200 carries `<Error>` on the first try, or a memory reading over 120 MB, goes back to Tim.
- **G2, resume data for a failed drag-out download** waits on D1. The download itself and its progress do not: the delegate path is Apple's documented one. If D1 shows no resume data or a resume that does not continue, the failure path drops the resume step and starts again (Drag-out, Failure), with no other change.

## URL commands `record-screen` and `stop-recording`

- **Names and where they hook in.** `URLCommand.Kind` gains `.recordScreen = "record-screen"` and `.stopRecording = "stop-recording"`, after `.captureText` and before `.restoreRecentlyClosed`. `URLCommandTarget` gains `startRecording(intent:)`, `startRecording(at: ScreenRect, intent:)` and `stopRecordingCommand() -> RecordingStopCommandOutcome`; `AppCoordinator` implements them in `+Recording.swift`, and `URLTargetRecorder` in the tests records them. The handler's switch routes `.recordScreen` to the first two (with a placement, the second) and `.stopRecording` to the third. CleanShot has no recording commands, so there are no aliases **(the design's call)**.
- **Kind rules.** `record-screen` is a capture (`isCapture` true): the capture drop applies to it, it counts as the first capture command of a launch burst, and it takes `action`. `stop-recording` captures nothing (`isCapture` false, `takesAction` false, `takesArea` false), so it is never dropped behind the lock, never sets `captureRan` in `ready()`, and runs whenever URL commands are on. The `capturesNothing` list in `URLCommandTests` gains it.
- **Parameters of `record-screen`.**
  - `action`: `upload` or `save`, with the meaning `CaptureIntent(urlAction:setting:)` gives them (`upload` is upload-only when that is the after-capture setting). `copy`, `annotate` and `pin` are refused at parse time with `Unknown action “<value>” for record-screen. Use upload or save.` **(the design's call)**: a recording ignores `copyOnly` and `then` (R1, `saveRecording`), and a sender asking for `copy` would otherwise expect the movie on the clipboard and get a save. `URLCommand.Kind.actions` lists what each kind takes (every action for the screenshot kinds, `[.upload, .save]` for `record-screen`), and `parse` checks the value against it. Without `action` the after-capture setting applies, as for every capture.
  - `x`, `y`, `width`, `height` and `display`, under 2.9's rules (`takesArea` true): all four or none, bottom-left origin, `display` 1-based and used only with the rect, resolved by `URLCommandGeometry.resolve` and clipped to the display. With a rect the recording starts at once with no selection (below), so `capturesWithoutSelection` is true for `.recordScreen` with an area, as for `capture-area`. The handler refuses a resolved rect under 32×32 pt before it reaches the target, with the `TidyShot URL command` notice `The area must be at least 32 by 32 points to record.` **(the design's call)**, so the recording's own refusal (`Select a larger area to record.`, written for a drag) is never shown for a URL. The rect is not written to `lastAreaRect`: a recording's area is never the previous area (R1).
  - Nothing else: no `display` alone (it is ignored with no rect, as for every command), no window, no duration, no format and no microphone switch. `record-fullscreen` is not in R4 (Deferred).
- **Gate.** Off refuses both with `URL commands are off. Turn them on in Settings > General.`. On, a plain `record-screen` runs: the selection shows, so the user's drag or click is the consent, as for `capture-area`. `record-screen` with a rect needs `Allow URL commands that capture without a selection` and is otherwise refused with the existing text, `record-screen from <sender> captures without a selection. Allow it in Settings > General > URL commands.`. `stop-recording` needs only the first toggle. The caption under the second toggle becomes `capture-fullscreen, capture-previous-area, and capture-area, capture-text or record-screen with x, y, width and height capture or record at once. Any app on this Mac, and any web page you let open TidyShot, can send them, and an allowed command lets the sending app read the result from the clipboard.`
- **Consent.** A URL start goes through the same code as the shortcut's start, so nothing is skipped: `recordingPrompts()` shows the microphone and camera prompts when access is undecided (and the system's Screen Recording prompt appears as today when the app has no grant), `recordingCanStart` checks the disk and resolves the input and the camera under the lock, the count-in runs when `Count down before recording` is on, and the bar, Stop and Cancel work as for any recording. Access already granted is used without a prompt, as for the shortcut. A `record-screen` with a rect records that rect after the prompts and the count-in; the silent toggle is what allows it, and its caption says so.
- **What `record-screen` does in each phase.** `recordScreen()` today toggles (`recordShortcutStarts`: a count-in is cancelled, a recording stopped). The URL command must only start, so the start is split out: `recordScreen()` keeps the toggle and then calls the new `startRecordScreen(intent:placement:)`, which the URL entry points call directly after a start-only check, `recordingStartsFromURL(phase:askingPermission:isQuitting:)` (pure). The lock's drop in the handler runs first, so most phases never reach it:
  - **idle**: starts. With no rect, the prompts, then the selection (`selector.select(mode: .recording, …)`), then the count-in and the recording, as the shortcut. With a rect, the prompts, then under the lock the content read, the display with the rect's `displayID` (gone: `Recording failed` / `The display is no longer connected.`, the driver's existing text), `RecordingConfiguration.isLargeEnough` as a backstop, then `record(.area(rect: display:), inputs:)`, so the count-in, the bubble, the rings and the bar are all as for a dragged area. The intent's action decides the save and the upload; `intent.sender` travels but recording notices do not name it, as today **(the design's call)**.
  - **selecting** (a recording's or a screenshot's selection is open, the lock held): dropped by the handler, logged `URL command from <sender> dropped, a capture is running: <url>`, no notice, as every capture command is today.
  - **count-in**: dropped, logged `… dropped, a recording is starting: <url>`. It does not cancel the count-in.
  - **recording**: dropped, logged `… dropped, a recording is running: <url>`. It does not stop the recording.
  - **saving** (a GIF converting, a mute ladder or a save running; the lock is free): starts a new recording, as the shortcut does.
  - **a prompt open** (`askingPermission`): dropped, `… dropped, the <microphone|camera> prompt is open: <url>`, as today.
  - **quitting**: nothing starts; `Recording: URL start ignored, the app is quitting` is logged.
  - For the two new reasons, `CaptureRefusal` gains `.recordingStarting` and `.recordingRunning`, and `captureRefusal(isCapturing:askingPermission:recordingPhase:)` gives them for `.countIn` and `.recording` before `.captureRunning` **(the design's call)**: today every drop during a recording says `a capture is running`, which is true but less useful in a log. `refusesCapture` and the status menu are unchanged. `dropReason` maps them to the two strings above.
- **What `stop-recording` does in each phase**, from the pure `recordingStopCommandAction(for phase:) -> RecordingStopCommandAction` (`.stop`, `.cancelCountIn`, `.nothing`):
  - **recording**: `recorder.stop()`. The recording ends as the bar's Stop ends it: the file is kept, saved and uploaded by the intent its start was given, and `Recording stopped` and the notes post as today. The outcome is logged `Recording: stop-recording from <sender> stopped the recording`.
  - **count-in**: `selfTimer.cancel()`, as the shortcut does **(the design's call)**: the sender asked for no recording, and a count-in that goes on would record after the stop. Logged `Recording: stop-recording from <sender> cancelled the count-in`.
  - **idle, selecting, saving, or a prompt open**: nothing. A recording's open selection stays (Escape cancels it); a GIF conversion goes on (`Stop Making GIF` is the status menu's). Logged `Recording: stop-recording from <sender> did nothing, no recording is running`. No notice **(the design's call)**: a macro that stops defensively should not post a banner each time.
  - A second `stop-recording` during the stop wait is a no-op (`RecordingController.end` guards on `isRunning` and the earlier cause), logged as nothing running.
  - `stop-recording` is not a toggle: it never starts anything.
- **Log lines**, in order for a run: the handler's `URL command from <sender>: <url>` (as today), then the recording's own lines, then for `stop-recording` the outcome line above. `RecordingLog` (`io.tky.tidyshot`, `Recording`) carries the `Recording:` lines; the handler's lines are `NSLog`, as today.
- **Notices.** No new notice except the two refusals above (`Unknown action … for record-screen`, `The area must be at least 32 by 32 points to record.`), both titled `TidyShot URL command` with `From <sender>` as the subtitle when known. The hold (R2, Decided 2026-10-24, 8) applies: a notice asked for during a URL-started recording waits for Stop.
- **README.** The URL scheme table gains two rows: `record-screen` (`Record Screen: select an area or a window; with a rectangle, record it at once (needs the second switch). action is upload or save`) with `action`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, `display`; `stop-recording` (`Stop the recording, or cancel its count-in; nothing otherwise`) with none. The Security paragraph names `record-screen` with a rectangle among the commands the second switch covers, and says a recording started by URL asks for the microphone and the camera as the shortcut does.

## Multipart upload

- **When.** `MultipartPlan.uses(payload:byteCount:)` is true for a `.file` payload (an MP4 or a GIF) of 100 MiB or more **(the design's call)**, read on the bytes actually sent (Clop's result when it was accepted), where `fitsSingleUpload` is read today. Screenshots and files under 100 MiB keep the single PUT. The threshold is below 5 GiB because a 3 GiB file that fails at 90 % today starts over on each of its six attempts, after hashing the whole file before it sends a byte; with parts, a failure costs one part. The cost is that most recordings take the new path, so it is tested against the stub (Tests) and against Tim's bucket (M1). A size that cannot be read counts as a single PUT, as today, and the queue then reports the file unreadable.
- **Parts.** `MultipartPlan.partSize(for byteCount:)`: 16 MiB, raised to `ceil(byteCount / 10,000)` rounded up to a whole MiB when 16 MiB would need more than 10,000 parts (from 156.25 GiB), and capped at 64 MiB **(the design's call)**. Every part but the last is the same size (Cloudflare R2 requires it); the last has no minimum. The part size is fixed at Create and stored, never recomputed. A file over 10,000 × 64 MiB = 625 GiB is refused: `RecordingSavePlan.oversizedNotice` becomes `Not uploaded` / `The recording is larger than 625 GB, too large to upload.`, and the refusal keeps R1's handling (`.none`, never `.failed`; a pending file moves into the save folder, a save-folder file stays), with `RecordingLimits.singleUploadBytes` and `fitsSingleUpload` replaced by `MultipartPlan.maxBytes` and `MultipartPlan.fits(byteCount:)`. No real recording reaches it (R1's disk limits stop a recording long before); the rule exists so the plan is total.
- **Concurrency.** The queue stays serial across jobs. Within a multipart job, 3 parts are in flight at a time (`MultipartPlan.inFlight`, a constant, not a setting) **(the design's call)**, in a `TaskGroup` that starts the next part as one finishes, in part order. Memory is at most 3 parts plus their hashing, about 48 MiB at 16 MiB parts and under 200 MiB at the 64 MiB cap.
- **Reading a part.** Each part is read into `Data` with a `FileHandle` (`seek(toOffset:)`, `read(upToCount:)`) in a `@concurrent` function, hashed with SHA-256, signed with that hash and sent with `session.upload(for:from:delegate:)` **(the design's call)** over the two alternatives (a temporary file per part, which copies the whole recording through the disk again; a streamed request, which has no async form and needs `needNewBodyStream` to resend). The bytes hashed are the bytes sent, so S3 rejects a part corrupted in transit, and the rule against loading a recording into memory still holds for the file as a whole. The save folder's security scope is held for the whole attempt, as `uploadFile` holds it for a single PUT today. A short read (the file shrank) throws `UploadError.fileUnreadable`.
- **Signing and requests.** `S3Destination` gains `signedRequest(method:key:query:payloadHash:extraHeaders:)` and `S3DestinationConfig.objectURL(for:query:)`, both built from one list of decoded `(name, value)` pairs, so the URL's query and the canonical query (`S3Signer.canonicalQuery`, already written and unused) cannot disagree; the existing overloads pass an empty list. `x-amz-content-sha256` is the part's hash, never `UNSIGNED-PAYLOAD` (a bucket policy may deny it); no `Content-MD5` and no `x-amz-checksum-*`. The six calls, path-style for Tim's dotted bucket:
  - `createMultipartUpload(key:contentType:cacheControl:) -> String`: `POST /<bucket>/<key>?uploads` with an empty body, the `content-type`, `cache-control` (when given) and `x-amz-acl: public-read` (when `sendPublicReadACL`) headers signed, as `putHeaders` does; the `<UploadId>` is read from the body;
  - `uploadPart(key:uploadID:number:data:progress:) -> String`: `PUT /<bucket>/<key>?partNumber=<n>&uploadId=<id>`; the `ETag` response header is the result; a 404 whose body code is `NoSuchUpload` throws `UploadError.noSuchUpload`;
  - `completeMultipartUpload(key:uploadID:parts:)`: `POST /<bucket>/<key>?uploadId=<id>` with `<CompleteMultipartUpload><Part><PartNumber/><ETag/></Part>…</CompleteMultipartUpload>` in ascending order, built from TidyShot's own records, never from ListParts; a 200 whose body holds `<Error>` throws `UploadError.httpStatus(200, body: excerpt)`, which is retryable; a 404 `NoSuchUpload` throws `.noSuchUpload`; `EntityTooSmall`, `InvalidPart` and `InvalidPartOrder` (400) are not retryable, as every 4xx;
  - `abortMultipartUpload(key:uploadID:)`: `DELETE /<bucket>/<key>?uploadId=<id>`; 204 and 404 both count as done;
  - `listParts(key:uploadID:) -> [MultipartPart]`: `GET /<bucket>/<key>?uploadId=<id>&part-number-marker=<m>`, paged until `IsTruncated` is false, giving number, ETag and size;
  - `objectSize(key:) -> Int64?`: HEAD, 200 with `Content-Length`, nil on 403 and 404.
  - The XML readers (`UploadId`, `Error`, `Part` lists) are small element scans in the style of `excerpt`'s `elementText`, and every error body still goes through `excerpt`, whose 200-character cap stays load-bearing. `UploadError` gains `.noSuchUpload` (`The upload was no longer known to the server.`, not retryable; the queue handles it apart, below). The `Destination` protocol gains these six, and `FakeDestination` scripts them.
- **What is persisted, and where.**
  - **On the record**, `CaptureRecord.multipart: MultipartUploadState?` (`decodeIfPresent`, so old history decodes), written once, in the same main-actor turn the queue reports Create (`UploadEvent.multipartStarted(id:state:)`, delivered before any part is sent): `uploadID`, `key`, `location: ObjectLocation`, `partSize`, `byteCount` (the source's size), `sourceModified: Date`, `sourcePath: String?` (the kept Clop copy's path, below; nil when the source is the record's own file) and `startedAt`. So Delete and pruning always know what to abort, after a quit or a crash too.
  - **In a sidecar**, `Application Support/TidyShot/multipart/<recordID>.json` (`MultipartStore`, a `nonisolated` `Sendable` struct next to `PendingFileStore`), `{parts: [{number, etag, size}]}`, rewritten atomically by the queue after each part completes. The record is not rewritten per part: `HistoryStore.save` rewrites all of `history.json` on every update, and a 50 GiB file has 3,200 parts.
  - **Clop's copy.** Clop's result lives in `tmp/Clop`, which launch removes. When multipart applies and the source is Clop's result, `startUpload` moves it to `multipart/<recordID>.mp4` before the enqueue, stores that path in `sourcePath`, and the job sends it from there; it is removed when the upload completes or the state is dropped **(the design's call)** over restarting from a fresh Clop run, which would throw the progress away and re-optimise. A move that fails logs `Upload: Clop's copy could not be kept for resume: <error>` and the job sends from `tmp/Clop` as today, with no resume across a relaunch.
  - **Cleared** on `.uploaded`: the record's `multipart`, the sidecar and the kept copy go. Kept on `.failed` and `.cancelled`.
  - **Launch sweep**: files in `multipart/` whose id has no record, or whose record has no `multipart` state, are removed, after `history.load`; nothing is aborted at launch (no network at launch, as no upload resumes by itself).
- **Progress.** `UploadProgressDelegate` gains a byte callback (`onBytesSent(delta:)`), and one `ProgressGate` serves the whole job: the fraction is the bytes of completed parts plus the bytes the in-flight parts have sent, over the file's size. The single PUT keeps its fraction callback. `UploadEvent.started` becomes `.started(id:attempt:fraction:)`: a resumed or retried multipart job starts at its done fraction, a single PUT at 0, and `handle`'s `attempt > 1` branch sets the bar to the event's fraction instead of 0. The card keeps `Uploading…` with its ring at the byte fraction, and the row's detail keeps `Uploading`; neither names parts **(the design's call)**. The `Uploading a recording` activity (idle sleep held off) is unchanged.
- **Failure and retry.** A part retries on its own under `RetryPolicy.file` (6 attempts, 2 to 30 s), with the same `isRetryable` rule; the other parts in flight carry on. When one part runs out of attempts the group is cancelled, the job ends `.failed(id:message:)` with `Part <k> of <n>: <error>` (for example `Part 12 of 200: Server returned HTTP 503.`), the record is `.failed` with that message, and `Retry` resumes (below). The upload id, the parts done and the kept copy all stay, so the failed job's work is not lost. A `.noSuchUpload` on a part ends the attempt and starts again under the same key (below), not as a failure.
- **Resume.** Retry (card and row), Retry Failed Uploads, and a relaunch's `HistoryStore.load` (which already turns `.uploading` into `.failed("Upload was interrupted.")` and now keeps `multipart`) all go through `retry(recordID:destination:)`, which passes the stored state and the sidecar's parts to the job as `UploadJob.resume`. No upload resumes by itself at launch, as today **(the design's call)**: a relaunch on a different network should not start sending gigabytes unasked. Before it resumes, the queue checks, through the pure `MultipartPlan.resumeDecision(stored:lane:source:sidecar:)`:
  - the stored `location` equals the lane's, and the stored `key` equals the key the job would use (the fixed key for a replace; for a template job, the stored key is reused with no new HEAD, counter or random draw);
  - the source has the stored size and modification date (`sourcePath` when set, the record's file otherwise);
  - then it sends only the parts the sidecar does not list, 3 at a time, and completes. Progress starts at the done fraction.
  - If the location, the key or the source differs, it aborts the stored upload (best effort, logged `Upload: <name> source changed, starting again` or `… destination changed, starting again`), drops the state, and starts a new upload; a template job then draws its key as a first upload does.
  - If the sidecar is missing or unreadable, `listParts` rebuilds it from the server, logged `Upload: <name> part list missing, listing parts`; a part the server lists with the stored part size is trusted, any other size is sent again. If ListParts fails (a 403 from a key without `s3:ListMultipartUploadParts`), the upload is aborted and started again (G1).
  - `noSuchUpload` from a part or from Complete's first try (the lifecycle rule or a provider removed it): the state is dropped, logged `Upload: <name> upload id gone, starting again`, and a new upload starts under the same key within the same attempt.
  - `noSuchUpload` from a retried Complete (the first Complete may have succeeded as its response was lost): `objectSize(key:)`; a size equal to `byteCount` counts as uploaded; anything else fails the job, not retryable.
  - **Clop on a resume.** A retry of a record with `multipart` state whose `sourcePath` still exists with the stored size and date skips Clop and resumes from that copy **(the design's call)**; today a Retry runs Clop again. With no kept copy, or one that no longer matches, Clop runs as today and the result starts a new upload.
- **Key.** As today: a template job reserves its key with HEAD before Create; a fixed key (the editor's replace, Trim's Save and Upload and `Upload Trimmed`) gets no HEAD and Creates at that key, where the old object keeps serving until Complete replaces it in one step. `Cache-Control: no-cache` for a replace goes on Create. `If-None-Match: *` on Complete is left out (support outside AWS is unconfirmed).
- **Trim's `Upload Trimmed` and Save and Upload on a large file** take the same path: `startUpload(fixedKey:)` plans multipart on the trimmed file's size, the record's `priorUpload` is kept as today, a failure returns the record to `.uploaded` at the old link with `uploadIsStale` and `multipart` kept, so a later `Upload Trimmed` resumes the replace, and the link shows the full recording until Complete.
- **Cancel and Delete.** `UploadQueue.cancel(recordID:)` cancels the group; each part task stops at its next check or its request's cancellation, and `cancel` waits for all of them before `.cancelled` is delivered, so no part lands after the abort. `.cancelled(id:reservedKey:)` is unchanged; the record keeps `multipart` from Create. `CaptureDeletion.aborts(for:) -> [MultipartRef]` lists the record's upload id with its key and location, and `deleteCapture` aborts it, through `abortMultipartUpload` signed with the credentials stored now at the stored location, before `deleteObjects` removes the keys as today (the key is still removed: a Complete that landed as it was cancelled made an object). 204 and 404 count as done; any other failure takes the existing path, the second alert with `CaptureDeletion.storageFailureMessage`, so a capture whose upload cannot be aborted is never stuck. Delete also removes the sidecar and the kept copy. `removeFiles(ofPruned:)` and `deleteOrphanedObject` abort the same way, best effort and logged.
- **Orphaned uploads.** The app aborts only upload ids it stored. There is no ListMultipartUploads sweep **(the design's call)**: Tim's upload-only key likely lacks `s3:ListBucketMultipartUploads`, and a bucket-wide sweep could abort another client's upload. Uploads lost to a crash before Create's event landed, or whose record was deleted while the app was not running, are the lifecycle rule's: `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` after 7 days on the whole bucket, a setup step in the README (Probes). Cloudflare R2 aborts incomplete uploads after 7 days by default. A resume after the rule fired gets `NoSuchUpload` and starts again.
- **S3-compatible providers.** Equal-size parts, a signed payload per part and no checksum work on AWS, R2, Backblaze B2 and MinIO; R2 ignores `x-amz-acl` on Create, which changes nothing. A provider with a lower object limit answers `EntityTooLarge` at Complete through the existing failure path.
- **Logged**: `Upload: <name> multipart, <n> parts of <m> MiB` at Create; `Upload: <name> resumes at <done> of <n> parts`; `Upload: <name> part <k> failed, retry <attempt>: <error>`; `Upload: <name> multipart completed`; `Upload: <name> multipart aborted`, `… abort failed: <error>`; the three "starting again" lines above. Never the upload id in a notice; the id is in the log only when Create fails (`Upload: <name> create failed: <error>`), which has none.
- **README.** The Screen recording section's sentence on the single PUT becomes: a recording of 100 MiB or more uploads in 16 MiB parts, three at a time; a failed part is sent again, and a failed or interrupted upload resumes from the parts already sent when you retry, also after a relaunch; a recording over 625 GB is kept and `Not uploaded` says why. The Upload section gains a `Large uploads` paragraph: the IAM actions an upload-only key needs for multipart (`s3:PutObject`, which covers Create, UploadPart and Complete; `s3:AbortMultipartUpload`; `s3:ListMultipartUploadParts` for a resume whose part list was lost), and the lifecycle rule as JSON for `aws s3api put-bucket-lifecycle-configuration`, with why (an upload never finished is billed until it is aborted; TidyShot aborts the ones it knows about, the rule the rest). The R1 checklist's 5 GiB line is replaced by R4's.

## Drag-out download

- **When it starts.** A time-based record (an MP4 or a GIF) with no local file and an empty drag folder, uploaded, not marked `Sound not muted`: today its card has no drag file and nothing downloads. In R4 the download starts when a drag on the card passes the 3 pt threshold with no `dragFileURL` (`QuickAccessCardDragSource.mouseDragged`, through a new `model.onDownloadForDrag`), not on the press **(the design's call)**: a press is also the click that opens the video (R1), so R1's reason stands, and the user has to move the card to ask for the file. Neither a restored card's appearance nor Restore Closed Overlay downloads a video (`prefetchDragFile` keeps its early return for `isTimeBased`). There is no menu item for it **(the design's call)**.
- **The download.** `AppCoordinator.downloadDragFile(recordID:)` in `+DragOut.swift`, under `downloadingDragIDs: [UUID: Task<URL?, Never>]` (new stored state beside `stagingVideoIDs`), one per record. It uses the signed GET from `downloadSource(of:title: nil)` **(the design's call)** over the public URL: one auth path, the one Save As… and Trim's download use, and a private bucket works; Copy Image already depends on `s3:GetObject`. The new `S3Destination.download(key:to:progress:)` is built on `session.downloadTask(with:)` with a task delegate (`UploadProgressDelegate` gains `didWriteData`, reporting `totalBytesWritten / totalBytesExpectedToWrite` through the same throttle), bridged with a continuation; the async `download(for:delegate:)` is not used, because it is reported not to deliver `didWriteData`. The expected size is `Content-Length`, and `byteCount` when the response has none. On a 2xx the temporary file is moved into `Drag/<id>/<name>` with `DragFileStore.adopt(id:name:moving:)` (a rename on the same volume), under the name `DragFileName.name(for:suggestion:)` gives, as every drag file. Redirects are refused and a non-2xx is the usual `UploadError`, as in `download(key:to:)`.
- **Progress.** The card's status becomes `QuickAccessStatus.downloading(Double)`, drawn like `.uploading`: the small circular ring with the text `Downloading…` and the file label under it. The coordinator pushes it through `QuickAccessController.setDownloading(_:for:)`, as it pushes `isOptimizing`; the status is the coordinator's flag, not a record field, because the record does not change. The row's detail reads `Downloading` while the task runs (`downloadingVideoIDs` in `RecentCaptureFormatting.detail`, as `isOptimizing` reads) **(the design's call)**. The auto-close countdown treats a running download as it treats `isDraggingOut`: the card stays while it downloads.
- **The hand-off.** When the file lands, `overlay.setDragFile(url, for:)`. If the button is still held, the existing wait in `mouseDragged` starts the session on the next mouse-dragged event; otherwise the next drag carries the file at once. No receiver ever sees a partial file: URLSession's temporary file is never on the pasteboard, and the move is a rename. This is 2.9's rule: the drag hands over a file URL to a copy in the container, and the session starts only once the file exists. The 60 s clean-up after the card closes and the removal on Delete apply to the downloaded copy as to any drag file; a card restored after the clean-up downloads again.
- **A press, a second drag and Save As… during a download.** A press runs `prepareDragFile` → `stageVideoDragFile`, which, with a download running, awaits it and hands its result to the card instead of reading the empty folder. A second drag past the threshold during the download starts nothing (`downloadingDragIDs` has the id) and waits as the first did. Save As… (`saveVideoCopy`) awaits a running download, then copies the landed file, so it does not start a second GET; with no download running it behaves as today.
- **Cancel.** Closing the card, for any reason, cancels the download (`overlayDidClose` cancels `downloadingDragIDs[id]` before `scheduleDragCleanup`), and so do Delete and the clean-up's `removeDragFolder`; a `dragGenerations` bump makes a download that finishes anyway drop its file, as `prefetchDragFile` does. There is no Cancel button on the card **(the design's call)**: the card's Close badge is the cancel. URLSession removes its own temporary file on cancel. Logged `Drag-out download for <id> cancelled`.
- **Failure.** The card shows `Download failed` (`QuickAccessStatus.downloadFailed(String)`, the error as the status's tool tip, no button), the drag folder stays empty, and nothing posts, as the image prefetch posts none. The next drag past the threshold tries again. The failed task's `NSURLSessionDownloadTaskResumeData`, when it has any, is kept in memory with its time (`dragDownloadResume: [UUID: (data: Data, at: Date)]`), and a retry within 10 minutes resumes from it with `downloadTask(withResumeData:)`; after that, or with none, it starts again **(the design's call)**, since S3 accepts the signed request for 15 minutes (G2). Resume data is dropped on Delete and when the card closes. Logged `Drag-out download for <id> failed: <error>`, as today's prefetch logs.
- **Disk space.** Before it starts, `RecordingLimits.hasRoom(for: byteCount, freeBytes: freeBytes(at: Drag/))` must hold (the file's size plus 1 GB); otherwise the card shows `Download failed` with `Your disk is almost full.` (`RecordingLimits.diskFullText`) as its tool tip and nothing is requested, logged `Drag-out download for <id> refused, not enough disk space`. An unreadable value, or a record with no `byteCount`, counts as enough, as elsewhere. The temporary file and `Drag/` are on the container's volume, so the move needs no second allowance; a disk that fills during the download fails the task, which is a failure as above.
- **Open, Copy File, Trim.** Unchanged: a thumbnail click opens the link, Copy File still needs a local file (a downloaded drag copy is not a local file; it is in `tmp`), and Trim downloads its own copy into `tmp/Trim` as today.

## The Settings prompt's deferral reason

`requestAccessFromSettings` logs why a prompt was deferred. Today the reason is `the <name> prompt is open` when a prompt is open and otherwise `a capture is running`; with only a save in flight (`recordingPhase == .saving`, `isCapturing` false, no prompt), as while a GIF converts, that is false. The pure `settingsPromptDeferralReason(phase:isCapturing:askingPermission:) -> String` gives, in order: `the <microphone|camera> prompt is open`; `a capture is running` when `isCapturing` (a count-in and a recording always hold the lock, so this stays their reason); `a recording is being saved` for `.saving`. The log line is `Recording: <microphone|camera> prompt from Settings deferred, <reason>`. Nothing else changes; the R3 checklist's line for the toggle during a recording stays true.

## The design's calls (for Tim to overrule)

1. No CleanShot aliases for the two commands (URL commands, Names).
2. `record-screen` takes `action=upload|save` only, refusing `copy`, `annotate` and `pin` with `Unknown action “<value>” for record-screen. Use upload or save.` (Parameters).
3. A rect under 32×32 pt is refused by the handler with `The area must be at least 32 by 32 points to record.` (Parameters).
4. A `record-screen` with a rect records at once behind the silent toggle, instead of preselecting a disarmed rect in the selection (Parameters, Gate).
5. Recording notices do not name the URL's sender (What `record-screen` does, idle).
6. `CaptureRefusal` gains `.recordingStarting` and `.recordingRunning`, and dropped commands log `a recording is starting` and `a recording is running` (What `record-screen` does).
7. `stop-recording` cancels a count-in (What `stop-recording` does).
8. `stop-recording` with nothing to stop posts no notice and only logs (What `stop-recording` does).
9. Multipart from 100 MiB, for every `.file` payload (Multipart, When).
10. Parts of 16 MiB, raised to `ceil(size / 10,000)` from 156.25 GiB, capped at 64 MiB; 625 GiB refused with `The recording is larger than 625 GB, too large to upload.` (Parts).
11. 3 parts in flight per job, a constant (Concurrency).
12. Parts are read into `Data` and sent with `upload(for:from:)`, not through temporary files or a stream (Reading a part).
13. Clop's result is kept in `multipart/<id>.mp4` for a resume, and a Retry with a kept copy skips Clop (What is persisted; Resume).
14. The card and row show no part count, only the byte fraction (Progress).
15. No upload resumes by itself at launch; Retry and Retry Failed Uploads resume (Resume).
16. No ListMultipartUploads sweep; the lifecycle rule is the backstop, as a README setup step (Orphaned uploads).
17. The drag-out download starts at the drag threshold, not on the press, and has no menu item (Drag-out, When it starts).
18. The download uses the signed GET, not the public URL (The download).
19. The row's detail reads `Downloading` during it (Progress).
20. No Cancel button: closing the card cancels the download (Cancel).
21. A failed download's resume data is used within 10 minutes, then dropped (Failure).
