# TidyShot screen recording R3: design (2026-10-24)

R3 adds click highlights and a webcam bubble to the screen recording built in R1 and R2. R1's design is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-tidyshot-screen-recording-design.md` and R2's is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-tidyshot-screen-recording-r2-design.md`; everything there still holds unless this document changes it. R2's decisions stand (R2, Decided 2026-10-23 and Decided 2026-10-24, 1 to 11). The research behind this design was done in `.superpowers/sdd/r3/`, which is local scratch and not in the repository; this document restates everything it uses from it. The probe sources are in `docs/superpowers/research/recording-probes/` (listed in its README under R3), and none has been run yet (Probes). There is no keystroke overlay in R3.

**Tim's decisions (2026-10-24).**
- **Features.** Click highlights and a webcam bubble. No keystroke overlay.
- **Bubble placement.** A round window on the screen that the recording films. It can be dragged anywhere, before or during recording. It starts in the recorded area's bottom-right corner, and its last place is remembered.
- **Bubble size.** A setting with Small, Medium and Large; Medium is the default. A double-click on the bubble steps through the sizes during recording.
- **Click highlights.** Left and right clicks, a short ring at each click, a different colour for right clicks. A drag keeps its ring until the button is released.
- **Controls.** Toggles in Settings → Capture → Recording, both off by default, plus a button on the recording bar for each that turns it on or off mid-recording.

Every choice below that these answers do not settle is marked **(the design's call)**, and the list at the end collects them so Tim can overrule any one. Sizes of strokes, log wording and type names are implementation details and are not marked.

## Who owns the bubble, the camera and the rings

- **`RecordingExtras`** (`TidyShot/Recording/RecordingExtras.swift`, main actor) owns, for one area or fullscreen recording, the bubble panel, the camera session, the ring overlay and the click monitors. `record()` makes it before `recordingHandOff`, for an area or fullscreen target only, and closes it in a `defer`, so every exit tears it down: a cancelled count-in (`selfTimer.run` returning other than `.finished`), a quit during the count-in (`finishRecordingForQuit` cancels it), a quit before the count-in began, a display or window gone, a failed content read, the `guard let start, !isQuitting` exit, and the normal end after `recorder.run` returns.
- **`RecordingScreenChrome`** holds a reference to it, so the bar's buttons reach it, and its `close` calls `extras.close()` as well, so on Stop the bubble goes with the bar. `close` is idempotent; the `defer` is then a no-op.
- **Nothing of R3 outlives a recording, or a start that gave up.** `close` hides both windows at once on the main actor, removes the monitors and every ring layer, removes the session's observers, and asks the camera queue to stop the session (Webcam bubble, The camera session). Then it closes the windows.
- **Window recordings** get no `RecordingExtras` at all (Filming, Window recordings).

## Filming the bubble and the rings

- **Two filmed windows.** `RecordingExclusions` gains `filmedWindows: Set<CGWindowID>`: the bubble panel and the ring overlay. In `makeFilter`, area and fullscreen recordings pass `coverWindows ∪ filmedWindows`, resolved through `OverlayWindowLookup`, as `exceptingWindows` of `SCContentFilter(display:excludingApplications: [own], exceptingWindows:)`. ScreenCaptureKit's header (`SCStream.h`, lines 155 to 186) says an excepted window of an excluded app is shown, which is how R1 films the desktop covers. R1's "What stays out" rule now has these two exceptions beside the covers; every other TidyShot window, the bar, the frame and tool tips included, stays out.
- **When they are made.** `RecordingExtras.show(at:)` creates both windows and orders them front at the start of the count-in, or, with the count-in off, right after the selection is dismissed, before the settle delay. The place it is given (`RecordingExtrasPlace`: the recorded rect, the ring area and the display's frames) comes from the target (the area's rect, or the display's `visibleFrame`), converted as `SelfTimerScreenStage` converts it. After the content read, the hand-off calls `extras.place(at:)` with the final place (an area is intersected with the display's fresh frame), which re-clamps the bubble if the rect changed, and reads `extras.windowNumbers` beside `chrome.windowNumbers`. Both windows are on screen before the lookup, which reads `onScreenWindowsOnly: true` and builds the filter once.
- **Both windows exist for every area and fullscreen recording, whatever the toggles say.** When a feature is off, its window is on screen and invisible: `backgroundColor = .clear`, `isOpaque = false`, `alphaValue = 1`, nothing drawn, `ignoresMouseEvents = true`. The bar buttons then only draw into them. G2 gates this. **(the design's call)** over `updateContentFilter`, which R2's `updateConfiguration` experience makes the riskier path.
- **The overlay draws nothing outside the recording.** It exists from the count-in, empty, so the lookup finds it; the monitors that feed it are installed only from `.recordingStarted` (Click highlights). The count-in's digits are at `.screenSaver`, as is the overlay, which holds no layer then.
- **Fallback filter** (`own` missing): `excludingWindows` lists only the chrome and the cards, so both windows show with no change.
- **Lookup misses.** `RecordingStreamDriver.start` returns the filmed windows the filter holds (`async throws -> Set<CGWindowID>`), in place of `Void`; the fallback filter returns both. `FakeRecordingDriver` returns what a test gives it. The controller passes the set to `chrome.setFilmed(_:)`. A feature whose window is missing is not shown: the bubble hides and its camera stops, or the monitors are never installed. That feature's bar button is disabled with the tool tip `Not available for this recording`, and the log line is `Recording: <bubble|highlights> window not in shareable content, not filmed`. When the feature was on at the start, a note posts after Stop: `Recorded without the webcam bubble` / `The bubble could not be filmed.`, or `Recorded without click highlights` / `The click highlights could not be filmed.` (Notices).
- **Window recordings** film one window (`desktopIndependentWindow`), so neither feature can appear. No `RecordingExtras` is made: no bubble, no overlay, no camera, no monitors, and the bar has neither button (Bar). The camera's resolution and note are dropped at the hand-off (Webcam bubble, Permission). Settings say so (Settings). **(the design's call)**: no switch to `includingWindows`, which would lose what R1 relies on `desktopIndependentWindow` for.
- **GIF** recordings get both in the MP4's pixels. Two things change in conversion:
  - **Reserved ring colours.** `GIFMaker` builds one global palette from 32 output frames spread evenly, so in a 60 s GIF at 15 fps a sample falls about every 1.9 s, and a 0.5 s ring is in a sample only about a quarter of the time. An unsampled ring maps to the nearest palette entry, with no dithering, and can lose its colour. So when highlights were on at any point in the recording, the palette reserves the two ring colours, yellow (255, 204, 0) and blue (10, 133, 255), and black, and the variance split fills the other 252 entries. `GIFPaletteBuilder.build(reserving:)` takes them; the controller's outcome says whether highlights were on, and the conversion passes the colours. The ring's 20 % fill and dark edge blend with what is under them and map to the nearest entry like any pixel.
  - **The bubble.** Its picture changes every frame, so each frame stores at least the bubble's box: GIFs with the bubble are larger and merge no frames. The face shares the one global palette, so it posterises as R2 noted for photos. Both are accepted, with a caption (Settings) **(the design's call)**, and checked by hand.
- **Teardown** is `RecordingExtras.close` (Who owns the bubble).

## Webcam bubble

- **Settings.** Capture → Recording gains, after `Highlight clicks`:
  - `Show webcam bubble`, a toggle, off by default, stored as `recordingWebcamBubble`;
  - `Camera`, a pop-up patterned after `Microphone` **(the design's call)**: Tim's answers do not mention choosing a camera, but his Mac has the Brio 500 and an iPhone, and `System Default` alone could pick either. Entries: `System Default` (default), then each camera by name from `AVCaptureDevice.DiscoverySession(deviceTypes: [.builtInWideAngleCamera, .external, .continuityCamera], mediaType: .video, position: .unspecified)` (G3). Desk View (`.deskViewCamera`) is left out, as a downward view is not a face **(the design's call)**. There is no `Off` entry; the toggle is the switch. Stored as `recordingCamera` (`default`, or the device's `uniqueID`) and `recordingCameraName` (the `localizedName`, written at the pick and refreshed whenever the list sees it). The list refreshes when the tab appears and on `AVCaptureDevice.wasConnectedNotification` and `wasDisconnectedNotification`. A stored camera missing from the list stays selected as `<recordingCameraName> (not connected)`, or `Unknown camera (not connected)` with no name;
  - `Bubble size`, a segmented picker `Small` / `Medium` / `Large`, Medium the default, stored as `recordingBubbleSize` (`small`, `medium`, `large`; anything else reads as `medium`).
  - `Camera` and `Bubble size` are disabled while `Show webcam bubble` is off **(the design's call)**. The pure `RecordingSettingsLayout` now takes the format and both R3 toggles (`init(format:clickHighlights:webcamBubble:)`) and gains `cameraRowsEnabled` (the bubble toggle), `showsBubbleGIFCaption` (GIF and the bubble toggle on) and `showsOverlayCaption` (either R3 toggle on). The captions are `A webcam bubble makes GIFs larger, because its picture changes in every frame.` under `Bubble size`, and `Click highlights and the webcam bubble apply to area and fullscreen recordings.` under the last R3 row.
- **Permission.**
  - **Entitlement and Info.plist**, in `project.yml` first, then regenerated: `com.apple.security.device.camera`; `NSCameraUsageDescription` = `TidyShot shows your camera in a bubble in screen recordings when you turn it on.`; `NSCameraUseContinuityCameraDeviceType` = true, or the iPhone reports as a built-in camera; `NSCameraReactionEffectGesturesEnabledDefault` = false, so a hand gesture does not fire balloons into a recording **(the design's call)**. Nothing else in the entitlements or the plist changes.
  - **Asking from Settings.** Turning `Show webcam bubble` on while the status is `.notDetermined` asks through the coordinator, not from the view: `AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess(for: .video)` with `askingPermission = .camera` set while the prompt is open, so every capture is refused then (Decided 2026-10-24, 9). While `recordingPhase` is not `.idle` or `isCapturing` is set, it does not ask: the toggle turns on, the log line is `Recording: camera prompt from Settings deferred, a capture is running`, and the next start's prompt asks instead. So the prompt is never filmed and never in a screenshot. The toggle stays on whatever the answer (Decided 2026-10-24, 7, carried over). Settings' microphone pick gets the same rule, closing the same gap in R2 **(the design's call)**.
  - **Denied.** While the toggle is on and the status is `.denied` or `.restricted`, the rows show `Camera access is off for TidyShot.` with `Privacy Settings…`, opening `x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Camera`. The status is read again when the Settings window becomes key.
  - **Before the capture lock.** A start with the toggle on and status `.notDetermined` (an ad-hoc rebuild can lose the grant) prompts before `withCaptureLock`, after the microphone prompt when both are due, so no prompt is filmed or in a count-in. A start with the toggle off never prompts. A GIF start skips the microphone prompt as R2 says, but not the camera prompt, because the bubble applies to GIF. Record Screen prompts before its selection, which cannot yet know whether the user will pick a window, so a window recording can follow a camera prompt it does not use **(the design's call)**; Record Fullscreen always uses it.
  - **The prompt state.** R2's `isAskingMicrophone` becomes `askingPermission: PermissionPrompt?` (`.microphone`, `.camera`; the enum lives in `AppCoordinator.swift` beside `refusesCapture`). The renames: `refusesCapture(isCapturing:askingPermission:)` and `captureRefusal(isCapturing:askingPermission:)`, refusing every capture while either prompt is open; `CaptureRefusal.microphonePrompt` becomes `.permissionPrompt(PermissionPrompt)`; `recordingShortcutAction(for:askingPermission:)`; `microphonePromptStep` becomes `permissionPromptStep(asksForAccess:isCapturing:)`, used for both prompts. The R2 log lines name the prompt: `… the microphone prompt is open` or `… the camera prompt is open` **(the design's call)**. Everything else in R2's Microphone, Access, holds for both.
  - **Under the lock**, beside `MicrophoneChoice.resolveAtStart`, the pure `CameraChoice.resolveAtStart(stored:access:devices:preferred:)` runs for every start, with the toggle on or off, so the bar can turn the bubble on; it takes no toggle, and whether its note posts is `CameraNote`'s call (When the note posts). It reads the access and the devices, which never prompts, and gives `CameraStart`: the camera's `uniqueID` and name, or none; the access; and a note for after Stop:
    - access not `.authorized`: no camera, the toggle kept, `Recorded without the webcam bubble` / `Camera access is off for TidyShot.`;
    - `default`: `AVCaptureDevice.systemPreferredCamera`, read once at the start; the bubble does not follow later changes to it **(the design's call)**;
    - a present device: that device, no note;
    - a missing device: the preferred camera, and `Recorded with <its name>` / `<stored name> is not connected.`;
    - no camera at all: no camera, and `Recorded without the webcam bubble` / `No camera is connected.` (`<stored name> is not connected.` when a device was stored).
  - **At the hand-off**, `CameraChoice.forRecording(_:source:)` drops the camera and its note for a `.window` source (the hand-off's `RecordingSource`, since `CaptureTarget` holds ScreenCaptureKit objects tests cannot make), as `MicrophoneChoice.forRecording` drops the input for a GIF.
  - **When the note posts.** The start's note posts only when the bubble was wanted: the toggle was on at the start, or the bar turned the bubble on during the recording. With the toggle off and the bar never pressed, nothing posts, whatever the access or the devices.
  - **`RecordingOptions`** carries the two toggles, the size, and the resolved camera as `cameraID: String?` and `cameraName: String?`, never the `AVCaptureDevice`, since it is a `nonisolated` `Sendable` struct. A Settings change mid-recording does not reach the run.
  - **The bar's button** (Bar) is enabled only when the start resolved a camera, access was `.authorized` and the bubble's window is filmed, so a mid-recording press can never raise the system prompt. The pure `BubbleButtonState.make(camera:access:filmed:)` (in `CameraChoice.swift`) gives its state and tool tip when disabled:
    - access `.notDetermined`: `Turn on Show webcam bubble in Settings to allow the camera.`;
    - `.denied` or `.restricted`: `Camera access is off for TidyShot.`;
    - no camera: `No camera is connected.`;
    - not filmed: `Not available for this recording`.
    - So until Settings has asked once, the bar cannot turn the bubble on **(the design's call)**: the bar is mid-recording, and the prompt would be filmed.
- **The window.** `WebcamBubblePanel`: an `NSPanel` with `[.borderless, .nonactivatingPanel]`, `isOpaque = false`, `backgroundColor = .clear`, `hasShadow = false` (a window shadow would be filmed as a dark halo), `hidesOnDeactivate = false`, never key or main, `level = .floating`, `collectionBehavior = [.canJoinAllSpaces, .fullScreenAuxiliary, .stationary, .ignoresCycle]`. Its content view returns true from `acceptsFirstMouse(for:)`, because TidyShot is not active and the panel is never key, so the first click of a drag or a double-click would otherwise be swallowed. It is layer-backed with `cornerRadius = side / 2` and `masksToBounds = true`, holding an `AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer(session:)` with `videoGravity = .resizeAspectFill` (the 16:9 frame cropped to the circle), `contentsScale` from the window's `backingScaleFactor`, updated in `viewDidChangeBackingProperties`.
  - **Above the bubble, the bar.** Both panels are at `.floating`, and a click on the bubble can raise it within that level. The bar is ordered front when it shows and again after each drag, size step and click on the bubble, so it stays above the bubble where they overlap.
  - **Sizes**, the circle's diameter in points: Small 120, Medium 180, Large 260 **(the design's call)**. At 2× Large is 520 px, so 720p is enough; 1080p costs USB bandwidth and decode for nothing visible.
  - **Format.** The session uses `sessionPreset = .hd1280x720` when `canSetSessionPreset` allows it, or else the device's format closest to 1280×720 that supports 30 fps. The frame rate is fixed at 30 fps by setting `activeVideoMinFrameDuration` and `activeVideoMaxFrameDuration` to 1/30 s under `lockForConfiguration`, because the preset sets only the size and a camera could otherwise run at 60 fps (G5).
  - **Border.** A 2 pt white stroke at 85 % opacity inside the circle's edge, drawn in a `CAShapeLayer` above the preview; no shadow, no name tag **(the design's call)**.
  - **Mirrored.** `automaticallyAdjustsVideoMirroring = false` and `isVideoMirrored = true` where supported, so the user sees a mirror and viewers see the same mirrored picture **(the design's call)**.
  - **Before the first frame** the circle is dark grey (white 0.15) with the border. **After a loss** (below) it shows `video.slash` in white at 40 % of the diameter on that grey.
- **Where it starts, and memory.** The pure `BubblePlacement.frame(recorded:size:insets:)` places the bubble inside the recorded rect (AppKit points: the area, or the display's `visibleFrame` for fullscreen) with its right and bottom edges `insets` from the rect's right and bottom edges, then clamps the whole bubble into the rect; a rect smaller than the bubble in either dimension centres it. The insets are stored as `recordingBubbleInsetRight` and `recordingBubbleInsetBottom`, 16 pt each by default **(the design's call)**; a stored value that is negative or not a finite number reads as 16. They are written when a drag ends and when a size step moves the bubble, from where the user left it, so the last place carries to the next recording whatever its area or display **(the design's call)**: one memory, not one per display. A clamp alone (a smaller area at the next start, or a size step against an edge) moves the bubble on screen but is not written back, so the memory does not drift.
- **Dragging.** The content view's `mouseDown` records the offset and `mouseDragged` moves the window, clamped so the whole bubble stays inside the recorded rect **(the design's call)**: a bubble outside the area would not be filmed, and a fullscreen bubble on the other display would not be either. For fullscreen the rect is the display's `visibleFrame`, so the bubble cannot cover the menu bar or the Dock, although both are filmed. `performDrag(with:)` is not used because it cannot clamp. The window is filmed where it is, so a drag changes nothing in the stream (B1 confirms a moved excepted window stays filmed). A right-click does nothing. Clicks on the bubble draw rings like any other click when highlights are on **(the design's call)**, through the local monitor (Click highlights).
- **Double-click** (`clickCount == 2`) steps Small → Medium → Large → Small, keeping the centre, then clamps. The new size is written to `recordingBubbleSize` **(the design's call)**, so the next recording starts at it, as its place does.
- **When it shows.** The area selection shows no bubble, because the area is not yet known **(the design's call)**; showing it once the rect is drawn would be the alternative. The bubble appears, with the camera starting, at the start of the count-in, or right after the selection when the count-in is off (Filming, When they are made). So "before recording" means two things here: the user can drag it during the count-in, and its place is remembered from the last recording **(the design's call)**. With the count-in off there is only the settle delay before the stream starts, so the bubble is placed by its memory or during the recording. There is no bar during the count-in, so the bubble cannot be hidden then except by cancelling.
- **The camera session.** One `AVCaptureSession` per recording with the resolved device's input and the preview layer as its only output; no audio input, no file output. The bubble's picture is filmed off the screen, so the camera never touches the `SCStream` or the `SCRecordingOutput`, and a camera failure never fails a recording.
  - **One queue owns it.** `CameraSession` (`nonisolated final class`, `@unchecked Sendable`) keeps the `AVCaptureSession`, its configuration, `startRunning`, `stopRunning` and every rebuild on one serial queue (`io.tky.tidyshot.camera`), and touches it nowhere else. The main actor sends it `run(true)` or `run(false)` and never waits. Each request bumps a generation; after `startRunning` returns, the queue stops the session again if the generation moved or the latest request was `run(false)`. So a Cancel, a Stop or a bar-off press while a start is still in flight (Continuity Camera can take seconds) still leaves the camera off. The pure `CameraRunState` holds that rule.
  - **Off is immediate on screen.** When the bubble turns off, or at `close`, the main actor hides the preview layer and the bubble's content at once, before the queue's stop, so no frame after "off" is filmed.
  - **Timing.** With the count-in on, the camera has the count-in's 3 s to start, so its first frames land before the stream's. With it off, it has only the settle delay, and the bubble may show its grey circle for the recording's first moments, longer for the iPhone.
  - **Loss mid-recording** (G4). The session's notifications arrive on its queue, which reads them against `CameraRunState` and hops to the main actor for the picture and the log. While the bubble is meant to be on, `AVCaptureSession.runtimeErrorNotification`, `wasInterruptedNotification`, `didStopRunningNotification`, or `AVCaptureDevice.wasDisconnectedNotification` for the device, shows the loss picture, keeps the bubble's place, logs the time, and the recording goes on. A stop TidyShot asked for (the bar's off, `close`) is not a loss: the request is recorded before `stopRunning`, and a stop notification while the bubble is meant to be off is ignored. The picture comes back on `interruptionEndedNotification` (the session restarts by itself after an interruption) or `didStartRunningNotification`. On `wasConnectedNotification` for the same `uniqueID` the session is rebuilt with it, only while the bubble is meant to be on; if it is off then, the rebuild waits for the next on. The pure `CameraRunState` holds these rules too.
  - **The loss note**, until C2 reports how each loss looks (G4): `Camera lost` / `The camera stopped at <m:ss>.`, followed by ` It came back at <m:ss>.` or ` The bubble had no picture from there.` One note, for the first loss. A session that fails to start gives `Recorded without the webcam bubble` / `The camera could not be started.`
  - **Access revoked mid-recording** behaves as a loss, whatever the system does to the session.
- **Bar button mid-recording** (Bar): on starts the session (if not running) and shows the bubble at its remembered place; off hides the picture at once, stops the session, and leaves the bubble invisible and click-through, its window kept on screen for the filter. The controller owns `showsBubble`, as it owns `isMuted`.
- **Logged**: `Recording: camera <name>, <width>×<height> at <fps> fps, started in <ms> ms`, `Recording: webcam bubble on|off at <m:ss>`, `Recording: bubble size <small|medium|large>`, `Recording: camera lost at <m:ss>: <notification>`, `Recording: camera back at <m:ss>`. Never the bubble's position.

## Click highlights

- **Setting.** `Highlight clicks`, a toggle after `Count down before recording` and before `Show webcam bubble`, off by default, stored as `recordingClickHighlights`. It needs no permission and no entitlement.
- **Seeing clicks.** `NSEvent.addGlobalMonitorForEvents(matching:)` for `.leftMouseDown, .leftMouseUp, .leftMouseDragged, .rightMouseDown, .rightMouseUp, .rightMouseDragged`. A global monitor does not see clicks on the app's own windows, so `addLocalMonitorForEvents` with the same mask adds clicks on the bubble (a TidyShot window that is filmed) **(the design's call)**; a local event on any other TidyShot window (the bar) draws nothing. The monitors are installed at `.recordingStarted` when the toggle is on, or when the bar button turns highlights on, and removed when highlights are turned off, on a lookup miss and in `close`. No event tap, no Accessibility or Input Monitoring grant (G1). A control-click is `leftMouseDown` with `.control`, and it gets the right-click colour, as apps treat it as a right-click **(the design's call)**.
- **The point.** A global event's `window` is nil and its `locationInWindow` is in screen coordinates; a local event on the bubble has `window` set and `locationInWindow` relative to it. So the screen point is `event.window.map { $0.convertPoint(toScreen: event.locationInWindow) } ?? event.locationInWindow`, in Cocoa coordinates, then flipped to CG (`cgY = NSScreen.screens[0].frame.maxY − cocoaY`). The pure `ClickHighlightRule.screenPoint(locationInWindow:windowFrame:)` does the first step (for a borderless window, the frame's origin plus the location), so both kinds are tested.
- **The overlay.** `ClickRingOverlay`: one borderless `NSPanel` covering the recorded display only, `ignoresMouseEvents = true`, `isOpaque = false`, clear background, no shadow, `.nonactivatingPanel`, never key or main, `level = .screenSaver` (set after `isFloatingPanel`, as `SelfTimerPanel` does), so rings draw over menus, the menu bar and the bubble; `collectionBehavior = [.canJoinAllSpaces, .fullScreenAuxiliary, .stationary, .ignoresCycle]`; `sharingType` left at its default. Rings are `CAShapeLayer`s with `contentsScale` from the screen; vector paths need nothing more for Retina. It holds no layers while highlights are off.
- **The ring** **(the design's call, size, strokes, colours and timings)**. 40 pt across: a 3.5 pt coloured stroke over a 6 pt dark stroke (black at 60 %), and a 20 % fill of the colour. Left: yellow, sRGB (1.0, 0.8, 0.0). Right, and control-click: blue, sRGB (0.04, 0.52, 1.0). Yellow against blue stays apart under the common colour-vision deficiencies; no shape cue. A fullscreen GIF of a 2560 pt display at 1280 px draws it 20 px across with a 1.75 px stroke and a 3 px edge, about the smallest that reads (P3 checks).
- **Animation**, from the pure `ClickRingTiming`:
  - on down: grow from 0.5× to 1× over 0.15 s, fully opaque;
  - while down: hold at 1×, and follow the pointer on each drag event, with implicit animations off (`CATransaction.setDisableActions(true)`), so the ring sits on the pointer instead of trailing it by about 0.25 s;
  - on up: once the grow has finished, expand to 1.3× and fade to clear over 0.35 s, then the layer is removed.
  - A quick click is visible for 0.5 s, and a drag from its down to its up plus 0.35 s. Frames showing a quick click, give or take one: GIF 10 fps about 5, 15 fps about 7, 20 fps about 10; MP4 30 fps 15, 60 fps 30. A ring shown only while the button is down (about 0.1 s) would show in 0 to 1 frames at 10 fps and 0 to 2 at 15, so a GIF could miss it.
  - Left and right can be down together; each button has its own ring.
- **Where rings draw.** `ClickHighlightRule` starts a ring on a down only if its CG point is inside the recorded display's frame and, for an area recording, inside the area's rect, converted to the overlay's coordinates on that display. A down outside draws nothing. On Tim's two displays only the recorded one has an overlay, so nothing shows on the other.
  - **A held ring** follows the pointer clamped to the recorded rect, so when a drag leaves the area or the display it waits at the nearest edge point and follows again when the pointer comes back.
  - **Its up ends it wherever the up lands**, inside the rect or not, or on the other display: ups and drags are matched to a held ring by button, never dropped for their position.
  - An up or a drag with no matching down (a button held when the monitors were installed) is ignored. No position is logged.
- **Nothing outside a recording.** The monitors exist from `.recordingStarted` to the end, never during the count-in or the selection. The overlay window exists from the count-in to the end (Filming), empty outside that span.
- **Bar button mid-recording** (Bar): on installs the monitors; off removes them, every held ring starts its fade at once as if its button had come up (the monitors will not see that up), and a ring already fading finishes **(the design's call)**. The controller owns `highlightsClicks`.
- **Logged**: `Recording: click highlights on|off at <m:ss>`. Never a click's position or count.

## Bar

- **Two buttons**, between the time and the mute button, for every area and fullscreen recording, on or off, and never for a window recording **(the design's call)**:
  - **Highlights**: `cursorarrow.rays` while on, `cursorarrow` while off; tool tips `Stop highlighting clicks` / `Highlight clicks`; accessibility labels the same. Disabled with `Not available for this recording` on a lookup miss.
  - **Bubble**: `video.fill` while on, `video.slash.fill` while off; tool tips `Hide the webcam bubble` / `Show the webcam bubble`; accessibility labels `Hide webcam bubble` / `Show webcam bubble`. Disabled with the tool tip from `BubbleButtonState` (Webcam bubble, Permission).
  - Order left to right: time, highlights, bubble, mute (or the `GIF` tag before the time), Stop, Cancel.
- **Widths.** `RecordingPanelLayout.Bar` becomes a struct (`showsTag`, `showsMute`, `showsOverlayButtons`), and the width is `172 + 36 × slots`, where the tag or the mute button is one slot and the two R3 buttons two: 172 and 208 for a window recording (as today), 244 for an area or fullscreen MP4 with no sound, 280 with sound or for GIF. Height 44, buttons 28 pt, spacing 6, leading 16, trailing 8, as today; `9:59:59` fits every bar. Everything that compared the bar with R2's three cases reads the fields instead: the tag's frame and `RecordingPanelModel.format` read `showsTag`, so an area or fullscreen GIF keeps its 30 s soft warning, and the mute button's frame reads `showsMute`.
- **State.** The controller owns both, as it owns mute.
  - **Initial state.** `highlightsClicks` is the highlights toggle. `showsBubble` is the bubble toggle and a usable camera together (`BubbleButtonState` enabled); with the toggle on and no camera, it starts false, showing the off symbol, disabled.
  - **The interface.** `RecordingChrome.show` gains `highlights: BarToggle` and `bubble: BarToggle` (`isOn`, `isEnabled`, `disabledToolTip`), with `onHighlights` and `onBubble`. `RecordingChrome` gains `setHighlights(_:monitoring:)` (the button's state, and whether the monitors are installed, which the controller makes true only from the first frame), `setBubble(_:)`, `setFilmed(_:)` and `filmedWindowNumbers`. `FakeRecordingChrome` records each call, and `FakeRecordingDriver.start` returns the filmed set a test gives it.
  - **Presses.** `toggleHighlights()` and `toggleBubble()` guard on `isRunning` and `cause == nil`, flip the value and call the chrome. A bubble press before the first frame applies at once, as the bubble has been on screen since the count-in; a highlights press before the first frame applies at `.recordingStarted`, when the monitors may be installed. A press once the recording is ending is ignored, and both buttons are disabled with Stop.
  - A bar press does not write Settings **(the design's call)**; the bubble's size step and place do (Webcam bubble).

## Budget and logging

- **CPU** **(the design's call, these numbers)**: the bubble under 5 % of one core in TidyShot's process at Medium with 720p30 (the preview layer is fed by the capture service; the camera's own cost sits in system processes), and click highlights under 1 %, checked in Activity Monitor during a minute of steady clicking.
- **Memory** **(the design's call)**: under 60 MB added while the bubble shows (a few 720p buffers and the layer), and under 5 MB for the rings, read with `footprint TidyShot` before and during. C2's numbers and the hand checks check these; over budget goes back to Tim (G5).
- **Latency**: the face lags by camera-to-screen time plus one screen frame. R2 measured the microphone 0.02 s (USB) to 0.13 s (iPhone) late, so lip sync is the difference between the camera's latency and the microphone's. It is measured in the hand checks with a clap in view of the camera and in reach of the microphone, reading the frame where the hands meet against the sound's onset (`mute-offset-onsets.py` reads the onset); it needs no new probe. Nothing compensates in R3.
- **Logged**: the lines under Webcam bubble and Click highlights, plus at the start `Recording: filming <n> own windows (<bubble, highlights>)`. No positions, no click counts, no frames.
