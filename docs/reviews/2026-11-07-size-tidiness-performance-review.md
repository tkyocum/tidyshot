# TidyShot size, tidiness and performance review, 2026-11-07

A read-only review of main at 754c0f7 for one question: where can the codebase get smaller, tidier or faster without changing what it does. Bugs were out of scope (the 2026-11-06 broad review covers them), as were style rules. TidyShot/ is 36,404 lines in 263 Swift files; TidyShotTests/ is 47,875 lines in 214 files holding 2,391 tests. Eleven independent passes read the code: nine by area (App; Editor model and rendering; Editor window and session; Recording core; Recording trim, mute and GIF; Upload and URL scheme; Scrolling and Capture; Selection, Overlay, Pin and SelfTimer; Output, History, RecentCaptures, Settings, Background, Desktop and Text), one across module seams, and one over the test suite. Three mechanical scans ran alongside: periphery for unused declarations, SwiftLint for size and complexity, and a normalised duplicate-block scan over both targets. Every finding kept below was then checked against the cited lines by hand; the agents' estimates of lines saved were kept where the code supported them and dropped where it did not. Nothing was built or run, so every timing is an estimate from the code and the specs' own measurements.

**Headline.** The app code is already lean in the ways that are hard to fix later: pure decision types with their own tests, I/O detached from the main actor, one drawing routine shared by canvas and export, frames copied out of their pixel buffers once. The verbatim-duplicate scan found only 39 repeated runs of five or more lines in 36k lines, most of them panel setup and the editor tools. What remains is (1) a handful of hot paths that redo work per frame or per mouse move, (2) parallel abstractions written twice for the same idea (two permission windows, two device adapters, two clocks, eleven borderless panels, two drag tools), (3) test seams and probes that outlived their reason, and (4) a test suite larger than the app that repeats one 90-line fixture block across three files. Summing the verified estimates with the overlaps removed: about 1,900 to 2,200 lines of app code (5 to 6 percent) can go at equal behaviour, plus the 276-line Debug-only spikes; the test suite can lose about 1,900 to 2,500 lines (4 to 5 percent) and 8 to 12 seconds of its 58-second run at equal coverage.

## The ten changes worth making first

Ranked by path heat times certainty for performance, and lines saved times certainty for size.

1. **OverlapFinder hashes the grey plane eight times per frame pair; two passes carry the same information.** [OverlapFinder.swift:59](TidyShot/Scrolling/OverlapFinder.swift:59), [:71](TidyShot/Scrolling/OverlapFinder.swift:71), [:112-113](TidyShot/Scrolling/OverlapFinder.swift:112). `rowHashes` is an FNV-1a chain over every grey byte of the given columns; the finder runs it over the usable columns, over the same columns split into 8 stripes, then over the moving columns striped and whole. Every result is used for equality and frequency only. Define a row's whole hash as FNV over its 8 stripe hashes (same equality classes), and when `moving.count == usable.count` (any page without a fixed sidebar) reuse the stripe and whole hashes from step 1. Passes over the 4.55 MB plane drop from 8 to 2 (4 with a sidebar). This is most of the documented 31 ms per 2560x1800 pair and most of why a Debug build cannot keep up (README, Build). Hot, high confidence on correctness, medium on the millisecond saving. Companions in the same file and folder: the grey conversion at [ScrollFrame.swift:28-31](TidyShot/Scrolling/ScrollFrame.swift:28) is a scalar per-pixel loop on the capture queue that `vImageMatrixMultiply_ARGB8888ToPlanar8` does in one call (vImage rounds where this truncates, so `ScrollFrameTests.swift:16` re-derives six values); the moving-column scan at [:100-108](TidyShot/Scrolling/OverlapFinder.swift:100) walks the plane column-major with a stride of `width` bytes and should go row-major with a still-columns bitset; the vote index at [:131-142](TidyShot/Scrolling/OverlapFinder.swift:131) builds a `[UInt64: [Int]]` per stripe per frame, about 10 to 15k small allocations, where positions fit a fixed 4-slot array and votes an array indexed by `dy + h`.

2. **SelectionView invalidates the whole display on every pointer move.** [SelectionView.swift:136](TidyShot/Selection/SelectionView.swift:136), [:171](TidyShot/Selection/SelectionView.swift:171), [:514](TidyShot/Selection/SelectionView.swift:514) set `needsDisplay = true`; `draw(_:)` at [:253-255](TidyShot/Selection/SelectionView.swift:253) refills the dim over `bounds` and, with Freeze on, blits the full snapshot clipped to a dirty rect that is the whole view. What changed is two 1 pt crosshair lines with halo, the 128 pt loupe, the size label and the drag rect's border. Keep the previous cursor, loupe and label rects and `setNeedsDisplay` the union of old and new strips and rects; keep whole-view invalidation for the rare paths (snapshot arrival, preset, adjust, exit). Also: `style(at:)` runs `Luminance.mean` per move, which creates a `CGColorSpace` and a 4 KB buffer each call ([Luminance.swift:19-22](TidyShot/Selection/Luminance.swift:19)); make the colour space a `static let`. And `SelectionController.swift:89` wires `onMouseMoved` in `.area` mode, where `updateHover` returns at once. Hot (60 to 120 Hz during every area or window selection). Visual-only risk: over-invalidate on the first cut.

3. **CanvasView redoes the tile and draw plan on every pointer move.** Four related items, all hot while drawing. (a) [CanvasView.swift:550-552](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:550) runs `syncTiles()` whenever `transient` changed, so a pencil or arrow drag re-runs it per mouse move although tile keys depend on effects alone; gate it on `now.document.effects != previous.document.effects || now.transient?.isEffect == true || previous.transient?.isEffect == true`. (b) [:546-549](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:546) reconciles the tile cache with the identical condition the model's `transient` didSet already applies ([EditorModel.swift:111-117](TidyShot/Editor/Session/EditorModel.swift:111)); delete the canvas copy. (c) `RedactionTileCache.currentTile` ([RedactionTileCache.swift:87-93](TidyShot/Editor/Rendering/RedactionTileCache.swift:87)) recomputes `orderedEffects` and the O(n²) `keys(for:)` for the whole document per redact per sync, although `reconcile` stored the same table in `wanted` two lines later; make it `tiles[id].flatMap { $0.key == wanted[id] ? $0.image : nil }`. (d) In `draw(_:)`, `backdropLayers` ([:826-832](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:826)) is evaluated twice (858 and 887) and calls `shownTile` per effect each time, `displayedEffects` up to four times, `displayedDrawings` and `displayedSpotlights` twice each; bind each once at the top of `draw` and keep an ordered tile list next to `shownTiles` so `backdropLayers` is `baseTiles + shownTileList`. Together these remove two O(n²) key computations per redact per frame and a full `BaseImageView.setTiles` relayout per pointer move.

4. **Stroke paths are rebuilt two to four times per stroke per frame.** `AnnotationGeometry.strokeBounds` ([ArrowGeometry.swift:115-119](TidyShot/Editor/Geometry/ArrowGeometry.swift:115)) builds the full Catmull-Rom path (8 samples per span) to read its bounding box and drops it; `CanvasView.draw` calls it for every drawing at [:917](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:917) and again for highlighters at [:852](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:852), builds the path once more for the backdrop clip at [:861](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:861), and [AnnotationDrawing.swift:326](TidyShot/Editor/Rendering/AnnotationDrawing.swift:326) builds it a last time to stroke. During a pencil drag `repaintRect` ([:584-586](TidyShot/Editor/Window/CanvasView.swift:584)) builds the transient's path per mouse move too. The model deliberately stores no `CGPath` (spec 2026-10-19 line 146, Sendable value types); a canvas-side memo keyed by annotation id and invalidated when `points` changes does not touch the model. Hot, medium-high confidence. Nearby and cheaper: `TextLayout` re-resolves the UI font up to six times per text box per draw ([TextLayout.swift:34-43](TidyShot/Editor/Geometry/TextLayout.swift:34)); `AnnotationDrawing` allocates two `CGColor`s per selection handle per frame ([:164-170](TidyShot/Editor/Rendering/AnnotationDrawing.swift:164)); `EditorToolbar.reflectColor` allocates a new swatch `NSImage` on every pan and zoom tick because one observation covers tool, colour and zoom ([EditorToolbar.swift:135-157](TidyShot/Editor/Window/EditorToolbar.swift:135)); `BaseImageView.layoutLayers` writes two constant filter properties on every layer on every transform change ([BaseImageView.swift:178-189](TidyShot/Editor/Window/BaseImageView.swift:178)).

5. **The GIF differ's palette cache collides; a direct table is exact and faster.** [GIFFrameDiffer.swift:76-85](TidyShot/Recording/GIF/GIFFrameDiffer.swift:76): every pixel of every stored frame does a multiply hash into a 65,536-slot direct-mapped table; a miss or collision runs the 255-entry linear scan in `GIFPalette.nearest`. The R2 spec measured mapping at 1.3 s of 4.2 s at 1280 px and 7 s of 15.7 s at full size, and says the cache "never changes a result", so any exact cache is byte-for-byte safe. Replace `cacheKeys`/`cacheValues` with one `[UInt8](repeating: 255, count: 1 << 24)` indexed by the 24-bit colour (255 is free: `nearest` returns at most 254): one byte load and compare per pixel, no hash, no collisions, 16 MB once per conversion against the 37.7 MB BGRA frames the reader already holds. If 16 MB is unwanted, `1 << 20` slots with the hash is a one-constant change. Hot per pixel; the GIF bytes do not change, so `GIFFrameDifferTests` and `GIFEncoderTests` pass as they are.

6. **HistoryStore pays a formatter per record and a full pretty-printed encode per mutation, on the main actor.** [HistoryStore.swift:162-178](TidyShot/History/HistoryStore.swift:162): both `JSONEncoder`/`JSONDecoder` date strategies are `.custom` closures that allocate an `ISO8601DateFormatter` per date, so every `save()` creates one formatter per record and every load one or two. `save()` ([:199-215](TidyShot/History/HistoryStore.swift:199)) encodes every record with `.prettyPrinted, .sortedKeys` and writes atomically, synchronously; the coordinator calls `add`/`update` from 27 sites, three or four times per capture (add, thumbnail name, `multipartStarted`, uploaded) and three per trim. Replace the closures with `Date.ISO8601FormatStyle(includingFractionalSeconds: true)`, a `Sendable` value type with the identical wire format (`2026-11-07T10:15:30.123Z`), and coalesce writes with a dirty flag and one save per main-actor turn, or snapshot `records` into a detached encode. Warm (several times per capture); high confidence it happens, medium on how much it costs with a retention-pruned history. `setProgress` is already in-memory, so the pattern is known here.

7. **Delete the two Debug spikes, or move them beside the other probes.** [RecordingSpike.swift](TidyShot/Recording/RecordingSpike.swift) (193 lines) and [ClickMonitorSpike.swift](TidyShot/Recording/Clicks/ClickMonitorSpike.swift) (83 lines) are whole-file `#if DEBUG` probes launched from [AppDelegate.swift:44-53](TidyShot/App/AppDelegate.swift:44) by two `UserDefaults` flags. Their results are recorded in the R1 and R3 specs, every other probe lives under `docs/superpowers/research/recording-probes/` uncompiled, and `RecordingSpike` re-implements the stream, output and delegate setup that `ScreenRecordingDriver` now owns, with a 10 ms polling wait at [:184-191](TidyShot/Recording/RecordingSpike.swift:184). Zero bytes in Release, 276 lines in every Debug build and in the app target. If they must stay re-runnable, a `TidyShotProbes` target in project.yml keeps them out of the app. Medium confidence only because this is Tim's call.

8. **`Annotation`'s twelve cases are walked in full by six functions that each touch one payload field.** [Annotation.swift:148](TidyShot/Editor/Model/Annotation.swift:148) `moved(by:)`, [:193](TidyShot/Editor/Model/Annotation.swift:193) `withID`, [:226](TidyShot/Editor/Model/Annotation.swift:226) `scaled(by:)`, [:285](TidyShot/Editor/Model/Annotation.swift:285) `withColor`, [:320](TidyShot/Editor/Model/Annotation.swift:320) `withLineWidth`, and [HitTester.swift:76-113](TidyShot/Editor/Geometry/HitTester.swift:76) `resized`. Seven payload types sit behind the twelve cases, so rectangle and ellipse, arrow and line, pencil and highlighter are written twice each and the three `Region` effects three times; about 215 lines differ only in the one line applied per payload. One private `mapping(box:segment:stroke:text:region:spotlight:counter:radius:block:)` helper with a single twelve-case switch brings each function to 6 to 10 lines. About 100 lines removed, no behaviour change, `AnnotationDocumentTests`, `HitTesterTests`, `CanvasToolTests` and `EditorModelTests` untouched.

9. **Three creation tools are one drag state machine written three times.** [RectDragTool.swift](TidyShot/Editor/Tools/RectDragTool.swift) (71 lines), [TwoPointTool.swift](TidyShot/Editor/Tools/TwoPointTool.swift) (69) and [PencilTool.swift](TidyShot/Editor/Tools/PencilTool.swift) (81) hold the same fields (`start`, `current`, a shown key, `shown`) and the same `mouseDown`/`mouseDragged`/`flagsChanged`/`mouseUp` bodies; a diff of RectDragTool:26-62 against TwoPointTool:24-60 differs in one field name on three lines. The duplicate-block scan found this cluster as the largest in the app. One `DragShapeTool<Key: Equatable>` with per-tool `key(start:current:input:canvas:)` and `shape(for:canvas:)` brings 221 lines to about 120; keep the three names as subclasses or typealiases because `CanvasToolTests` asserts `tool is RectDragTool`.

10. **Eleven borderless panels repeat the same setup, and the "isFloatingPanel first" rule is copied into five of them.** `SelfTimerPanel`, `PinPanel`, `RecordingPanel`, `WebcamBubblePanel`, `ClickRingOverlay`, `DesktopCoverPanel`, `PreviewPanel`, `ScrollCapturePanel`, `QuickAccessPanel`, `ScrollAreaFrameWindow`, `SelectionWindow` each set `isFloatingPanel`, `level`, `hidesOnDeactivate = false`, `isOpaque = false`, `backgroundColor = .clear`, `hasShadow`, `collectionBehavior`, `animationBehavior`, `isReleasedWhenClosed = false` and override `canBecomeKey`/`canBecomeMain` (see [PinPanel.swift:6-24](TidyShot/Pin/PinPanel.swift:6)). One `OverlayPanel: NSPanel` base taking `level`, `collectionBehavior`, `hasShadow`, `animationBehavior` and `ignoresMouseEvents` as init parameters (level must stay a parameter: `PreviewPanel` and `DesktopCoverPanel` document why they are not floating) and a `configureAsOverlay` extension for the two `NSWindow`s saves about 55 lines net app-wide and puts the ordering rule in one place. `ClickRingOverlayTests`, `WebcamBubblePanelTests` and `RecordingPanelLayoutTests` assert resulting properties and keep passing.

## Performance: the rest of the hot and warm paths

- **Upload signing.** [S3Signer.swift:238-240](TidyShot/Upload/S3Signer.swift:238) `byteHex` builds a 16-element `[Character]` and a two-character `String` per digest byte, three digests per request, about 200 heap allocations per part; one `hex(_:)` over a static alphabet with reserved capacity removes them. [:226-231](TidyShot/Upload/S3Signer.swift:226) creates a `DateFormatter` and parses its pattern per signature; `Date.ISO8601FormatStyle` with omitted separators gives `20130524T000000Z` as a value type, and `S3SignerTests.swift:16` plus the SigV4 suite pin the output. [S3DestinationConfig.swift:94-156](TidyShot/Upload/S3DestinationConfig.swift:94): `objectURL`, `objectPath` and `effectivePathStyle` each call `normalized`, so a signed request normalises the config four or five times (string trims and a template split/join each) although `S3Destination.init` stored it normalised once. `ClopOptimiser.optimise` opens two `CGImageSource`s over the same bytes for type and size ([ClopOptimiser.swift:240-252](TidyShot/Upload/ClopOptimiser.swift:240)).
- **Recent Captures menu.** [RecentCaptureMenuItemView.swift:25-31](TidyShot/RecentCaptures/RecentCaptureMenuItemView.swift:25) calls both `detail` and `detailWithoutSize`, each of which runs `detailParts` and so allocates a `RelativeDateTimeFormatter` and a `ByteCountFormatter` ([RecentCaptureFormatting.swift:40-43](TidyShot/RecentCaptures/RecentCaptureFormatting.swift:40), [:65](TidyShot/RecentCaptures/RecentCaptureFormatting.swift:65)): 60 formatters per 15-row menu open. One `details(for:)` returning both strings halves it; a static formatter is awkward because `locale` is a parameter and the type is nonisolated.
- **Recording post-processing.** GIF frame scaling goes through `VTCreateCGImageFromCVPixelBuffer` and `CGContext.draw` with `.high` interpolation and a colour match per source frame ([GIFMaker.swift:230-238](TidyShot/Recording/GIFMaker.swift:230)); `vImageScale_ARGB8888` straight from the locked pixel buffer into a BGRA canvas would drop the `CGImage` creation and the colour-match pass, but it changes pixel values by a level or two and the spec tuned the palette on CG output, so measure on `p-4096` first (low-medium confidence). The settle loop in [AutoScrollDriver.swift:162-172](TidyShot/Scrolling/AutoScrollDriver.swift:162) sleeps a fixed 50 ms although the settler knows the instant it settles, adding 0 to 50 ms per auto-scroll step; and the driver re-hashes the frame it just hashed as the next baseline ([:141](TidyShot/Scrolling/AutoScrollDriver.swift:141), [:164-175](TidyShot/Scrolling/AutoScrollDriver.swift:164)), about 1.5 M FNV steps per step.
- **ScrollDiag in Release** is deliberate and documented (NSLog redacts in Release, users report with the log file); its per-frame cost is 15 to 20 `Mutex` reads and two eager `ContinuousClock.now` calls, on the order of microseconds. An `Atomic<Bool>` for `enabled` and an `if ScrollDiag.enabled` around the `compared` call is the whole change; not worth doing on its own.
- **Three cold polls that an event would replace.** [AppCoordinator.swift:510-512](TidyShot/App/AppCoordinator.swift:510) spins at 50 ms until `recordingPhase == .idle` during quit; [Notifier.swift:108-111](TidyShot/Output/Notifier.swift:108) at 10 ms until `releasedInFlight == 0`; [AppCoordinator+Upload.swift:88-90](TidyShot/App/AppCoordinator+Upload.swift:88) at 500 ms until the old lane is idle. A continuation resumed from the `didSet`, or `UploadQueue.waitUntilIdle()`, replaces each. The quit and lane polls are injected-clock driven and documented in the harness design, so this is the least valuable item here. The other 13 `Task.sleep` sites are UI delays, permission polls (TCC has no notification) or the capture algorithm itself.
- **Checked and fine.** Thumbnails are cached in memory (64 + 8) and on disk; history reads are in-memory; PNG and TIFF encoding, background composition, file moves and attribute reads are all detached; progress is throttled through one `ProgressGate`; a part is hashed once and sent from the same bytes; the recording hot path is empty (ScreenCaptureKit writes frames, the bubble is a preview layer fed off-main, click rings only set `position`); the stitcher appends rows into one reserved `Data` and `finish()` wraps without copying.

## Size: the same thing written twice, across modules
